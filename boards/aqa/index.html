AQA GCSE Physics revision

For AQA GCSE Physics (8463) and Combined Science (8464), Foundation and Higher tier.

Every lesson below is free. When you start, choose AQA, your course and your tier, and Upthrust shows only what is on your course.

WS Working scientifically

  • Variables and hypotheses

    I can say which variable I change, which I measure and which I keep the same, and how far a set of results backs up a hypothesis.

  • Accuracy, precision and repeat readings

    I can read a scale to its resolution, pick the instrument that will show the change I am looking for, and say whether a set of readings is accurate, precise, repeatable or reproducible.

  • Errors and uncertainty

    I can tell a random error from a systematic one, correct a reading for a zero error, deal with a reading that does not fit the rest, and give a result as a mean with its uncertainty.

  • Evaluating a method and its data

    I can find the mean, range, median and mode of a set of results, put results in a frequency table and read a histogram, and say what is wrong with a method and what would improve it.

4.1 / 6.1 Energy

  • Energy stores and transfers

    I can say where the energy is, where it went, and which of the four pathways it took to get there.

  • Kinetic energy

    I can work out how much energy a moving object has, and say what happens to it when the speed changes.

  • Gravitational potential energy

    I can work out how much energy goes into a thing's gravitational potential energy store when it is raised, using how far it actually went up, and I can say where that energy is when it comes back down.

  • Power

    I can work out a power by dividing the energy transferred by the time in seconds, and I convert the units before I divide.

  • Conservation and dissipation

    I can say that the total energy has not changed, and still explain why less of it is useful.

  • Reducing unwanted transfers

    I can say what makes a building cool faster or slower, and I can name a change that cuts an unwanted transfer instead of one that just uses the machine less.

  • Efficiency

    I can work out an efficiency the right way up, give it as a decimal or a percentage, and I know my answer is wrong if it comes out above 1.

  • Energy resources

    I can argue about energy resources instead of listing them: which are replenished, which can be relied on, what each one costs, and why the answer is not the same for everybody.

4.2 / 6.2 Electricity

  • Charge and current

    I can say what a current is (charge flowing, counted per second) and work out how much charge has gone past in a given time.

  • Circuit diagrams, symbols and meters

    I can read a circuit diagram, draw one with the right symbols, and put an ammeter and a voltmeter where they belong.

  • Potential difference, the volt and E = Q V

    I can say that potential difference is the energy given to each coulomb of charge, and work out the energy from the charge and the potential difference.

  • Resistance and V = I R

    I can say what resistance is, predict whether the current goes up or down when something in the circuit changes, and use V = I × R in any direction.

  • Investigating resistance

    I can set up a circuit to measure a resistance, take the readings without letting the wire heat up, and plot the results to show how the resistance depends on the length.

  • I–V characteristics

    I can look at a current against potential difference graph and say what the component is doing: whether its resistance holds steady, and if not, which way it moves and why.

  • Thermistors, LDRs and sensing

    I can say which way a thermistor and an LDR change, follow that through to the current, and explain a circuit that uses one to switch something on.

  • Series and parallel circuits

    I can say which quantity is the same and which is shared out in each arrangement, work out the readings in a single loop, and explain what happens when one component changes.

  • Combining resistors

    I can say which way each arrangement moves the total resistance, explain both in terms of what the charge can do, and use an equivalent resistance to work a circuit out.

  • Electrical power

    I can work out a power from a p.d. and a current, or from a current and a resistance, and I know which of the two a question has given me.

  • Energy transferred by appliances

    I can work out the energy an appliance transfers from its power rating and the time it runs for, and I convert the time into seconds before I multiply.

  • The National Grid

    I can explain that raising the potential difference for the grid makes the current in the cables smaller, and that a smaller current reduces the heating of the cables, so less power is wasted.

  • Mains electricity

    I can say what the mains supply in the UK is, and explain the difference between it and a battery in terms of which way the charge is pushed.

  • Plugs, wiring and safety

    I can say what each of the three mains wires does and its potential difference from earth, and explain why a live wire is dangerous even when the switch is open.

  • Charging by friction

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain how rubbing two surfaces together charges them both, and work out which one ends up negative from which way the electrons went.

  • Electric fields

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain what an electric field is, read the strength and the direction off a field drawing, and use the idea to explain a force acting across a gap.

4.3 / 6.3 Particle model of matter

  • The particle model: solids, liquids and gases

    I can say how the particles are arranged in each state, and use that arrangement to explain why the three densities are so different.

  • Density

    I can work out the density of something from its mass and its volume, and say why a bigger piece of it has the same density.

  • Measuring density

    I can measure the mass and the volume of a solid or a liquid myself, and write the method down in a way that scores.

  • Changes of state and conservation of mass

    I can say what happens to the mass when something melts, evaporates or sublimates, and I can explain in particles why it is a physical change and not a chemical one.

  • Internal energy

    I can look at something being heated and say where the energy has gone: into making the particles move faster, or into pulling them apart, and I can say which, because I can see what the thermometer is doing.

  • Specific heat capacity

    I can say how much energy it takes to warm something up, because I know how much of it there is, what it is made of, and how far I want its temperature to move.

  • Specific latent heat and heating curves

    I can look at a flat section on a heating or cooling graph and say what it is: a change of state, costing energy the whole way across while the thermometer stands still. And I can work out how much that energy is.

  • Gas pressure and particle motion

    I can start from a gas getting hotter and get all the way to its pressure rising, saying out loud what the molecules do at every step (faster, then more often, then harder) instead of jumping from one end of it to the other.

  • Gas pressure and volume

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say why giving a gas more room lowers its pressure, and work out a new pressure or a new volume when a fixed amount of gas is squashed or allowed to expand.

  • Doing work on a gas

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain why doing work on a gas makes it hotter, all the way from the force that pushes it to the speed of its particles.

4.4 / 6.4 Atomic structure

  • The atom, and how big it is

    I can describe what an atom is made of, say where the mass is, and say how tiny the nucleus is against the atom around it.

  • Atomic number, mass number and isotopes

    I can read a nuclear symbol, count the protons and neutrons in it, and say what makes two atoms isotopes of the same element.

  • How the model of the atom changed

    I can tell the story of the atom from the solid sphere to the nucleus, say what the scattering experiment showed, and explain why that changed the model.

  • What unstable nuclei emit

    I can say why a nucleus gives out radiation, name each thing it can give out, and say what each one is.

  • Penetration, range and ionising power

    I can say what stops each radiation, which ionises most, and pick the right one for a job by what it has to get through.

  • Nuclear equations

    I can complete a nuclear equation by making both rows add up, and say what each emission did to the nucleus.

  • Half-life and the randomness of decay

    I can say what a half-life is, find one from a graph or from numbers, and explain why decay is random for one nucleus and predictable for many.

  • Background radiation, and measuring it

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what background radiation is and where it comes from, and I take it off every reading before I trust a count.

  • Contamination and irradiation

    I can say whether something is contaminated or irradiated by asking where the radioactive material is, and compare the two hazards.

  • The hazards of ionising radiation

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain why a source's hazard depends on its half-life, and I never call a half-life right without naming the job.

  • Uses of nuclear radiation

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say how radiation from a radioactive source is used to see inside a body and to destroy unwanted tissue, and choose the radiation and the half-life that fit the job.

  • Nuclear fission and nuclear power

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what happens when a uranium nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits, why that can run on as a chain reaction, and what holds it steady in a reactor.

  • Nuclear fusion

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what nuclear fusion is, where its energy comes from, and how it differs from fission.

  • Modelling radioactive decay

    I can use dice to model random decay, plot the results as a decay curve, read a half-life off it, and say where the model stops.

4.5 / 6.5 Forces

  • Forces as arrows

    A force is an arrow: its length is the size and the way it points is the direction. I can name it, and say whether the two objects are touching.

  • Weight and gravity

    I can work out the weight of something anywhere, as long as I know its mass and how hard gravity pulls there.

  • Centre of mass

    I can mark the one point a weight arrow should start from, and say what that point means.

  • Resultant forces

    I can add every force on an object into one resultant, and say which way it points.

  • Distance-time graphs

    I can read a journey off a distance-time graph, and find how fast something was going at any point on it.

  • Units, prefixes and standard form

    I can give the SI unit of a quantity, say what each prefix means as a power of ten, and work with numbers in standard form.

  • Acceleration

    If I know three of starting speed, final speed, time, distance and acceleration, I can work out the one that is missing.

  • Velocity-time graphs

    I can get two things off a velocity-time graph: how fast it is speeding up, from the steepness, and how far it has gone, from the area underneath.

  • Newton's first law

    If the forces on something are balanced, its velocity does not change. It stays still if it was still, and keeps going if it was going.

  • Newton's second law

    I can work out any one of resultant force, mass or acceleration when I am given the other two.

  • The trolley practical

    I can plot a set of readings, fit a line to them, say what it shows, and say what I would change to make the readings better.

  • Newton's third law

    I can point at both objects in a pair and say which force acts on which.

  • Falling and reaching a steady speed

    I can explain, step by step, how a falling object ends up at terminal velocity.

  • Momentum

    Higher tier

    I can work out momentum, and explain what happens to it when things collide or push apart.

  • Force and change in momentum

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can work out a force from a change in momentum, and explain how a safety feature makes that force smaller.

  • Work done

    I can work out the work done by multiplying the force by how far the thing moved in the direction of that force, in metres.

  • Stopping distances

    I can say which distance a factor changes, and which way it changes it.

  • Braking and energy

    I can say where a braking car's energy goes, naming the store it leaves and the store it ends up in.

  • Springing back, or not

    I can say whether something will spring back when the force is taken off, and why.

  • Springs and the spring constant

    I can work out how stiff a spring is from the graph of force against how far it stretches.

  • Moments

    GCSE Physics only

    I can work out a moment, say which way a beam turns, and say why a longer spanner shifts a stiff nut.

  • Levers and gears

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say why moving a force further from the pivot makes it easier to turn something.

  • Pressure on a surface

    GCSE Physics only

    I can work out the pressure under something, using the area that is actually touching.

  • Pressure in a liquid

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can work out the pressure at a depth in a liquid, and say why something floats.

4.6 / 6.6 Waves

  • Transverse and longitudinal waves

    I can say what a wave carries and what it leaves behind, and I can tell a transverse wave from a longitudinal one by what moves and which way.

  • Describing a wave

    I can define all four of amplitude, wavelength, frequency and period, and read the first two off a diagram without doubling or halving either of them.

  • The wave equation

    I can use the wave equation in any of its three arrangements, and I convert the units before I divide rather than afterwards.

  • Measuring wave speed

    I can measure the speed of a wave two different ways, and I can say which of them a given wave needs and why.

  • Waves at a boundary

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what happens to a wave at a boundary, and I draw the normal before I measure any angle.

  • Refraction

    I can say which way a wave bends at a boundary and why, and I get the sound cases right as well as the light ones.

  • The electromagnetic spectrum

    I can name the seven groups in order, say what every one of them has in common, and explain how a wave moves energy from one place to another without moving anything material.

  • Uses of electromagnetic waves

    I can give a use for every group of the electromagnetic spectrum, and name the group rather than the gadget when a question asks me which one is being used.

  • Dangers of electromagnetic radiation

    I can say which parts of the spectrum can be hazardous to the body, and explain the harm at the level of a cell rather than in general words.

  • Where electromagnetic waves come from

    I can say where an electromagnetic wave was made: from a change in a nucleus, from electrons losing energy, or, for radio waves, from a current oscillating in a circuit.

  • Infrared, and what a surface does with it

    I can describe the surface investigation, say what has to be kept the same, and write a conclusion about emitting or absorbing without drifting into the other one.

  • Black bodies and the temperature of the Earth

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain why everything emits radiation, what changes when it gets hotter, and why an object sitting at a steady temperature is still absorbing and emitting as hard as ever.

  • Reflection, colour and filters

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain why an object is the colour it is, work out what it will look like under coloured light, and say what a filter has done to the light that came through it.

  • Lenses

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what each kind of lens does to the light reaching it, draw the rays that find the image, and say whether that image is real or virtual.

  • Sound and hearing

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what happens to the speed, the frequency and the wavelength of a sound when it crosses into a different material, and which one of the three cannot change.

  • Using waves to see inside things

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain how a pulse sent into something and the echo that comes back are used to find what is inside it, in a body, under a boat and inside the Earth.

4.7 / 6.7 Magnetism and electromagnetism

  • Magnets, poles and magnetic materials

    I can say which poles attract and which repel, and tell a permanent magnet from an induced one.

  • Magnetic field patterns

    I can draw the field of a bar magnet and read its direction and its strength from the lines.

  • Plotting compasses and the Earth's field

    I can plot a magnet's field with a compass, say why a compass points north, and use that to show that the Earth's core is magnetic.

  • The magnetic field around a wire

    I can show that a current makes a magnetic field, draw that field as widening circles, and find which way it goes from the current.

  • Solenoids and electromagnets

    I can explain how a solenoid makes a strong magnetic field, and say how adding an iron core increases the strength of its magnetic field.

  • Electromagnetic devices

    GCSE Physics only

    I can read a diagram of an electromagnetic device, even one I have never seen, and explain step by step how it works.

  • The motor effect and Fleming's left-hand rule

    Higher tier

    I can explain the force on a wire in a magnetic field, and find which way it acts with my left hand.

  • Calculating the force on a wire

    Higher tier

    I can work out the force on a wire in a magnetic field, and rearrange the equation to find B, I or l.

  • Electric motors

    Higher tier

    I can explain how the forces on the two sides of a coil make it turn, say which way it turns, and make it turn faster or the other way.

  • Electromagnetic induction

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can say when a p.d. is induced and when it is not, what makes it bigger, what reverses it, and why the induced current always pushes back.

  • Alternators and dynamos

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain why an alternator gives a.c. and a dynamo gives d.c., and what turning either one faster does to its output.

  • Loudspeakers and microphones

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain how a loudspeaker turns a varying current into sound, and how a microphone turns sound back into a varying current.

  • How a transformer works

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain how a changing current in one coil makes a p.d. in another, why it needs a.c. and an iron core, and how the turns make it step up or step down.

  • Transformer calculations

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can find any p.d. or number of turns on a transformer, with the primary on top of both sides, and check my answer goes the right way.

4.8 Space physics

GCSE Physics only

  • The solar system

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what is in our solar system and what orbits what, and that the Sun is a star, far bigger than any planet.

  • Gravity and orbits

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what orbits what, draw the force that keeps a satellite in orbit, and compare planets, moons and artificial satellites.

  • Orbital speed and orbit radius

    Higher tierGCSE Physics only

    I can explain how gravity changes a satellite's velocity without changing its speed, and why a smaller orbit has to be a faster one.

  • How a star forms

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain how gravity turned a cloud of dust and gas into a star, and why the star then stays the same size.

  • Red-shift

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain why the light from a galaxy moving away is red-shifted, and read from two spectra which galaxy is moving away faster.

  • The expanding universe and the Big Bang

    GCSE Physics only

    I can explain how the speeds and distances of galaxies show the universe is expanding, and why that means it began very small, hot and dense.

  • The life cycle of a star

    GCSE Physics only

    I can put the stages of a star's life in order, and say why it moves from each one to the next.

  • Theories of the universe

    GCSE Physics only

    I can say what the evidence tells us about how the universe began, why scientists trust it, and what it still cannot explain.
