Atomic structure: GCSE Physics revision

14 short interactive lessons. Every answer gets instant feedback and a "Why?".

Open Upthrust

Not every lesson is on every course. When you start, Upthrust shows only the lessons on yours.

  • The atom, and how big it is

    By the end, you can say:

    I can describe what an atom is made of, say where the mass is, and say how tiny the nucleus is against the atom around it.

  • Atomic number, mass number and isotopes

    By the end, you can say:

    I can read a nuclear symbol, count the protons and neutrons in it, and say what makes two atoms isotopes of the same element.

  • How the model of the atom changed

    By the end, you can say:

    I can tell the story of the atom from the solid sphere to the nucleus, say what the scattering experiment showed, and explain why that changed the model.

  • What unstable nuclei emit

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say why a nucleus gives out radiation, name each thing it can give out, and say what each one is.

  • Penetration, range and ionising power

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what stops each radiation, which ionises most, and pick the right one for a job by what it has to get through.

  • Nuclear equations

    By the end, you can say:

    I can complete a nuclear equation by making both rows add up, and say what each emission did to the nucleus.

  • Half-life and the randomness of decay

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what a half-life is, find one from a graph or from numbers, and explain why decay is random for one nucleus and predictable for many.

  • Background radiation, and measuring it

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what background radiation is and where it comes from, and I take it off every reading before I trust a count.

  • Contamination and irradiation

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say whether something is contaminated or irradiated by asking where the radioactive material is, and compare the two hazards.

  • The hazards of ionising radiation

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say how ionising radiation harms cells and why a source's hazard depends on its half-life.

  • Uses of nuclear radiation

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say how radiation from a radioactive source is used to see inside a body and to destroy unwanted tissue, and choose the radiation and the half-life that fit the job.

  • Nuclear fission and nuclear power

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what happens when a uranium nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits, why that can run on as a chain reaction, and what holds it steady in a reactor.

  • Nuclear fusion

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what nuclear fusion is, where its energy comes from, and how it differs from fission.

  • Modelling radioactive decay

    By the end, you can say:

    I can use dice to model random decay, plot the results as a decay curve, read a half-life off it, and say where the model stops.
