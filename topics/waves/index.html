Waves: GCSE Physics revision

16 short interactive lessons. Every answer gets instant feedback and a "Why?".

Open Upthrust

Not every lesson is on every course. When you start, Upthrust shows only the lessons on yours.

  • Transverse and longitudinal waves

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what a wave carries and what it leaves behind, and I can tell a transverse wave from a longitudinal one by what moves and which way.

  • Describing a wave

    By the end, you can say:

    I can define all four of amplitude, wavelength, frequency and period, and read the first two off a diagram without doubling or halving either of them.

  • The wave equation

    By the end, you can say:

    I can use the wave equation in any of its three arrangements, and I convert the units before I divide rather than afterwards.

  • Measuring wave speed

    By the end, you can say:

    I can measure the speed of a wave two different ways, and I can say which of them a given wave needs and why.

  • Waves at a boundary

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what happens to a wave at a boundary, and I draw the normal before I measure any angle.

  • Refraction

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say which way a wave bends at a boundary and why, and I get the sound cases right as well as the light ones.

  • The electromagnetic spectrum

    By the end, you can say:

    I can name the seven groups in order, say what every one of them has in common, and explain how a wave moves energy from one place to another without moving anything material.

  • Uses of electromagnetic waves

    By the end, you can say:

    I can give a use for every group of the electromagnetic spectrum, and name the group rather than the gadget when a question asks me which one is being used.

  • Dangers of electromagnetic radiation

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say which parts of the spectrum can be hazardous to the body, and explain the harm at the level of a cell rather than in general words.

  • Where electromagnetic waves come from

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say where electromagnetic waves are made: changes in atoms and nuclei, and oscillating currents in electrical circuits for radio waves.

  • Infrared, and what a surface does with it

    By the end, you can say:

    I can describe the surface investigation, say what has to be kept the same, and write a conclusion about emitting or absorbing without drifting into the other one.

  • Black bodies and the temperature of the Earth

    By the end, you can say:

    I can explain why everything emits radiation, and what changes about that radiation when an object is made hotter.

  • Reflection, colour and filters

    By the end, you can say:

    I can explain why an object is the colour it is, work out what it will look like under coloured light, and say what a filter has done to the light that came through it.

  • Lenses

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what each kind of lens does to the light reaching it, draw the rays that find the image, and say whether that image is real or virtual.

  • Sound and hearing

    By the end, you can say:

    I can say what happens to the speed, the frequency and the wavelength of a sound when it crosses into a different material, and which one of the three cannot change.

  • Using waves to see inside things

    By the end, you can say:

    I can explain how a pulse sent into something and the echo that comes back are used to find what is inside it, in a body, under a boat and inside the Earth.
